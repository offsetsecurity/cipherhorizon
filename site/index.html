Quantum-safe before it's urgent.

Post-quantum readiness for the protocols your organisation runs on: TLS, SSH and, next, VPN. A deep scan of each, a readiness level from L0 to L4, and one CycloneDX CBOM, self-hosted. It runs on your network and sends nothing anywhere.

Get started How it judges

Post-quantum readiness, protocol by protocol

A level from L0 to L4 for every TLS endpoint and SSH server, and which ones would leak recorded traffic to a future quantum computer. Systems ranked for migration by how long their data must stay secret.

Scanners that show their work

A TLS scanner: every protocol from SSLv2 to TLS 1.3, every cipher suite and group, STARTTLS for seven protocols, 55 cited checks. An SSH auditor that never logs in: it judges each server against a cited baseline and writes the sshd_config lines to meet it.

An inventory you can hand over

TLS endpoints, SSH servers and machines in one CycloneDX 1.7 CBOM. Two readable scripts inventory a machine without installing anything. PDF reports an auditor accepts.

Why now

NIST IR 8547 deprecates the quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms after 2030 and disallows them after 2035. Traffic recorded today with a classical key exchange can be decrypted once a large quantum computer exists: harvest now, decrypt later. The fix for TLS exists today (the hybrid key exchange X25519MLKEM768 in TLS 1.3); the hard part is knowing where you stand across every endpoint and machine, and keeping track. That is what CipherHorizon is for.
