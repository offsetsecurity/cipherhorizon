Methodology

Two measures, both this project's own, both with their working shown. Neither is a letter grade or another scanner's scale.

Post-quantum readiness, L0 to L4

LevelMeaning
L0 Not readyNo TLS 1.3, so no post-quantum key exchange is possible
L1 ExposedTLS 1.3, but no post-quantum key exchange offered: recorded traffic is exposed
L2 Hybrid availableA hybrid group is accepted, but a current client does not end up using it
L3 Hybrid negotiatedA current client negotiates a hybrid post-quantum key exchange
L4 PQ-authenticatedL3 plus a post-quantum certificate signature (ML-DSA)

Only standardised groups count (FIPS 203 ML-KEM in the IANA TLS registry); pre-standard draft groups are reported but do not raise the level.

TLS hygiene score, 0 to 100

Start at 100 and take off points for each distinct problem: critical 40, high 20, medium 10, low 4, info 0. A critical finding caps the score at 29 and a high one at 74, so a serious problem cannot be averaged away. Bands: Strong (90+), Sound (75+), Fair (55+), Weak (30+), Broken. Post-quantum findings are not counted: readiness answers that separately.

What is checked

55 checks in eight areas: certificates and trust (the chain against bundled root stores), revocation (OCSP stapling, Certificate Transparency), protocols, cipher suites, key exchange (groups, DH strength, post-quantum), features (renegotiation, compression, fallback protection), HTTP security (HSTS) and published attacks inferred from the configuration. Each check cites its sources: RFC 8446, RFC 9325, RFC 8996, NIST SP 800-52, FIPS 203/204, NIST IR 8547, IANA.

What is not checked

  • Behavioural vulnerabilities (Heartbleed, ROBOT): the scanner sends no exploit traffic.
  • Live revocation: the CA's OCSP responder and CRL are not contacted; a scan talks only to its target.
  • Anything behind client certificates, or on another network.
